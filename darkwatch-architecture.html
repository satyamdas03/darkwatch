Darkwatch

A radar contact with no transponder is either noise, a rig, a mismatch — or a ship that chose to disappear. Darkwatch decides which, and says how sure it is.

// 00

The gap

Ships are legally required to broadcast their position over AIS. The ones doing the most harm — illegal fishing, sanctions running, smuggling — simply switch it off and vanish from the cooperative picture. But radar doesn't need their cooperation. Sentinel-1 SAR sees through cloud and dark, and sees metal on water whether it wants to be seen or not. Cross the two, and the ships that went dark light up by their absence.

$23B+

Estimated annual value of illegal, unreported fishing alone — before smuggling and sanctions evasion.

Free

Sentinel-1 SAR: full global archive, open license, redistribution allowed, S3 + REST API access.

Solved→Open

Detecting the blip is solved. Deciding what it means is the open problem — and the heart of this build.

// 01

Architecture

Five stages, from raw radar to a defensible accusation. The easy stages are the two ends. The value — and the real research — lives in the middle, where two noisy, heterogeneous signals get fused and a contact gets a verdict it has to earn.

cooperative / transponding (AIS) radar return (SAR) uncertain / needs more evidence dark vessel — the catch
S1SAR Ingestion & Prepraw scenes → analysis-ready tiles

Pull Sentinel-1 scenes over a chosen maritime box, calibrate, and tile them into something a detector can chew. Fully open data, API-accessible — no wall here.

Scene fetch

Copernicus / AWS S3 by area + date. Cloud-optimized GeoTIFF.

Radiometric prep

Calibrate, speckle-filter, land-mask so only water remains.

Tiling

Chip huge scenes into GPU-sized tiles with geo-coordinates kept.

Ocean-only mask

Coastlines & fixed infrastructure removed up front.

S2Vessel Detectiontiles → candidate contacts

Find every metal-on-water return. This is the well-studied part — ship detection in SAR has strong published baselines. Fine-tune one on your 5060; don't reinvent it. The output is a list of contacts with position, size estimate, and a detection confidence.

Detector

Fine-tuned SAR ship detector (open baselines exist). Not a giant model.

Contact extraction

Each blip → lat/lon, length estimate, heading hint, timestamp.

Artifact filter

First pass at rejecting wave clutter, azimuth ambiguities, wind streaks.

Confidence score

How sure are we this is even a vessel? Carried forward, not discarded.

S3Fusion & Attributioncontacts + AIS → matched or dark

The heart of the system. Take every SAR contact and ask: is there an AIS-broadcasting ship that plausibly was right there, right then? Match them, and what's left unmatched is your candidate dark fleet. But "unmatched" is a claim with consequences, so this stage is really about reasoning under uncertainty, not a simple join.

DEEP PROBLEM

Spatiotemporal match

AIS is time-sampled and drifts; SAR is one instant. Match across a moving, uncertain gap.

Probabilistic association

Not yes/no. P(this contact = this AIS track), with the uncertainty made explicit.

Dark candidate finder

Contacts no AIS track can claim — ranked by how confidently they're truly dark.

Explain-away engine

Could it be a rig, a known false-return zone, an AIS gap not a switch-off? Rule those out first.

▶ THE PROBLEM THAT MAKES THIS RESEARCH, NOT PLUMBING

A missed match and a real dark vessel look identical at the blip level. AIS drops out for innocent reasons all the time; a SAR contact can be a wave. So the true task is calibrated inference: assign each contact an honest probability of being a deliberate dark vessel, fusing two signals that disagree about time, position, and even existence — and be right often enough that an accusation means something. This is the same honest-uncertainty problem at the center of the finance idea, wearing a radar's clothes.

S4Behavior & Intentdark contacts → why it matters

A single dark blip is weak. A dark blip loitering over a known fishing ground, or meeting another vessel at sea (a possible transshipment), or sitting in a protected marine area, is a signal worth acting on. This stage adds the context that turns a detection into an alert.

Pattern-of-life

Cross-scene: is this contact dark repeatedly, or once? Persistence = intent.

Zone overlay

Protected areas, EEZ lines, known fishing grounds, sanctioned waters.

Rendezvous flag

Two vessels meeting mid-ocean — the transshipment fingerprint.

Priority score

Rank alerts so a human looks at the 5 that matter, not 500 blips.

S5Alert & Evidenceeverything → a case a human can act on

Package each alert as something an analyst, patrol, or NGO can actually use and trust: the imagery, the reasoning, the confidence, the ruled-out alternatives. An accusation nobody can audit is worthless — the evidence trail is the product.

Contact dossier

SAR chip, position, time, size, behavior, confidence — one glance.

Why-dark rationale

Plain statement of what was ruled out and how sure the verdict is.

Map & replay

Where, when, and the track context around the detection.

Export

Feed to whoever acts — patrol, insurer, fishery authority, researcher.

// 03

Build plan

Sequenced so every phase ships something real on a single RTX 5060, on open data, with no licensing wall. The hard research (S3 fusion) comes as soon as there's something to fuse — because that's the make-or-break, and you want to hit it early.

PH0

Recon & first light

// get real SAR onto your screen
  • Register for Copernicus Data Space; pull a few Sentinel-1 scenes over one busy strait
  • Get comfortable with the format: calibrate, view, land-mask by hand once
  • Pick your test theater — one bounded sea area with known traffic
Ships: real radar imagery on disk, and a felt sense of the raw material.
LICENSE: Sentinel-1 open, redistribution OK. Zero blocker.
PH1

Ingestion pipeline

// S1 — scenes to tiles, automatically
  • Automate fetch → calibrate → speckle-filter → land-mask → tile
  • Keep geo-coordinates attached to every tile (you'll need them to match AIS)
  • Make the data source a swappable adapter from day one
Ships: point it at a box + date, get analysis-ready ocean tiles.
LICENSE: clean.
PH2

Vessel detector

// S2 — find every contact
  • Fine-tune an open SAR ship-detection baseline on your 5060
  • Output contacts with position, size, and a real confidence score
  • First-pass artifact rejection (waves, ambiguities)
Ships: a scene in, a clean list of vessel contacts out.
LICENSE: open training data exists; verify each set's terms as you go.
PH3

Fusion & attribution ★

// S3 — the research core. make-or-break.
  • Ingest AIS for the same box + time; reconstruct where each ship plausibly was
  • Build probabilistic association: P(contact = track), uncertainty explicit
  • The key test: on cases you can ground-truth, are your "dark" calls actually dark?
  • Calibrate until a stated p=0.9 means it's really dark ~90% of the time
Ships: the nameable contribution — calibrated dark-vessel attribution from fused SAR + AIS.
LICENSE: free historical/sampled AIS is fine for the build; global live feeds may cost — swappable adapter covers it.
PH4

Behavior & prioritization

// S4 — turn detections into alerts
  • Persistence across passes, zone overlays (MPAs, EEZs, fishing grounds)
  • Rendezvous detection for possible transshipment
  • Priority scoring so humans see the few that matter
Ships: ranked alerts with context, not a flat list of blips.
LICENSE: zone data mostly public/gov open.
PH5

Evidence layer & proof

// S5 — make it usable and provable
  • Contact dossiers: imagery + reasoning + confidence + ruled-out alternatives
  • Validate against a known case (a documented dark-fleet event) end to end
  • Write up the attribution method — publishable independent of any product
Ships: the whole chain, radar → auditable accusation, with evidence it works.
LICENSE: results & method publishable; raw data redistribution follows each source's terms.
